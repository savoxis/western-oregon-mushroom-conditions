How these numbers are made
Score
A 0 to 100 estimate of how aligned the weather is for a mushroom to fruit in a zone: soil temperature, recent rain, season timing and more. It estimates conditions, not what is in your basket.
Prime and go-now
Prime is a score of 60 or more. Go-now is 80 or more.
History
Past years are a reconstruction from weather reanalysis scored with the same model, not observed mushrooms. Live scores use a forecast model whose soil temperature differs slightly, so compare today with history loosely.
Scores against real reports
Public reports (iNaturalist, Mushroom Observer and similar, through GBIF) compared with the model: the timing of reports against the season curve, how often a mushroom is reported on high-score days, where reports come from by elevation and forest type, and how the weather history's soil temperature compares with measured soil temperature. Rough checks on modest samples; details and limits.
Next 7 days and best days
The same model, run each morning on the weather forecast (soil temperature and moisture, rain, frost) instead of past weather. Today's bar matches today's score. Forecasts change, so treat later days as a guide and look again before you go.
Checked against finds
We compare the scores with public reports of each mushroom. The match is clearest for fall king boletes, modest for chanterelles, weak for spring kings, and not shown for natural morels (whose score covers forest morels only). Details and limits.
Season window
The median first and last day a zone reached prime across complete years. At least 3 prime seasons are needed.
Odds by week
The share of complete years in which any day in a 7-day week reached prime. The band is an 80% Wilson interval, which stays honest with few years.
Elevation trend
A least-squares line of the typical best day on zone elevation. The r2 figure says how much of the spread elevation explains.
Totals across zones
Yearly totals use only the zones with the full record so years compare fairly. Newer zones with 3 years of history appear in the zone views, marked as a short record.
Not covered
Burn scars, permits and what has actually been reported on the ground. Check permit rules for the land unit before you pick.

Never eat any wild mushroom you have not positively identified yourself. Scores predict fruiting conditions, not what is actually in your basket. When in doubt, throw it out, or run finds past the Cascade Mycological Society (Eugene) or Central Oregon Mushroom Club (Bend). Check permit rules for the land unit before picking.
